# ENA Statistical Indicators

The table below summarizes the main statistical indicators currently used in the ENA workflow and `statistical_summary.json`.

| Indicator | Calculation | Theoretical / Typical Range | How to Interpret |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mean Point | The arithmetic mean of all projected ENA coordinates for a group on a given dimension. | No fixed universal range; depends on the projection and dataset. | Describes the average location of a group in ENA space. Larger separation between group mean points suggests stronger structural differences between groups. |
| Median Point | The median of projected ENA coordinates for a group on a given dimension. | No fixed universal range; depends on the projection and dataset. | Provides a robust summary of group location when the point distribution is skewed or contains outliers. |
| 95% Confidence Interval of Group Points | Estimated interval around the group mean point, usually computed as `mean ± t * SE` for each dimension. | No fixed universal range; depends on the projection and sample variability. | Shows uncertainty around the group mean location. Less overlap between groups suggests clearer separation in ENA space. |
| Welch t-test (`t`) | `t = (M1 - M2) / sqrt((s1^2 / n1) + (s2^2 / n2))`, using unequal-variance degrees of freedom. | Unbounded; can be positive or negative. | Tests whether two groups differ on a projected ENA dimension. Larger absolute values indicate stronger evidence of a difference. |
| *p* value | Probability of observing a test result at least as extreme as the one obtained under the null hypothesis. | `0` to `1`. | Smaller values indicate stronger evidence against the null hypothesis. In many studies, `p < .05` is treated as statistically significant. |
| Degrees of Freedom (`df`) | For Welch t-test, estimated with the Welch-Satterthwaite formula. | Positive real number. | Indicates the effective sample information used by the test. It supports the interpretation of the test statistic but is not usually interpreted substantively on its own. |
| Standard Deviation (`SD`) | `SD = sqrt(variance)` for group coordinates on a dimension. | `0` to positive infinity. | Indicates how dispersed a group's ENA points are around the mean. Larger values mean more variability within the group. |
| Cohen's *d* | `d = (M1 - M2) / pooled SD`. | Unbounded; can be positive or negative. Typical benchmarks are about `0.20` small, `0.50` medium, and `0.80` large. | Measures the standardized magnitude of group difference on an ENA dimension. The sign indicates direction; the absolute value indicates effect size magnitude. |
| Mann-Whitney *U* | Based on the rank ordering of two groups' values rather than their means. | `0` to `n1 × n2`. | Non-parametric test of whether two groups differ on a projected ENA dimension. Useful when normality assumptions are questionable. |
| Effect Size *r* for Mann-Whitney | Often approximated as `r = |Z| / sqrt(N)` or a closely related rank-based effect measure. | `0` to `1` when reported as an absolute value; can be `-1` to `1` if direction is retained. | Indicates the strength of a rank-based difference. Values closer to `0` indicate weak separation; larger values indicate stronger differences. |
| One-way ANOVA (`F`) | `F = between-group variance / within-group variance`. In a two-group setting, it parallels the t-test. | `0` to positive infinity. | Tests whether groups differ on a projected ENA dimension. In two-group ENA comparisons, it is best treated as companion evidence to the t-test. |
| Chi-square (`χ²`) | `χ² = Σ ((Observed - Expected)^2 / Expected)` across a contingency table. | `0` to positive infinity. | Tests whether code frequencies differ across groups. This is a frequency-based comparison and should not be interpreted as a direct test of ENA point separation. |
| Goodness of Fit / Co-registration Correlation | Correlation between projected ENA points and fitted network centroids, often reported as Pearson and Spearman correlations for each dimension. | `-1` to `1`, though useful ENA fit values are typically positive and high. | Indicates how faithfully the plotted ENA visualization reflects the underlying model geometry. Values closer to `1` suggest stronger visual fidelity. |
| Explained Variance Ratio | `eigenvalue_i / sum(eigenvalues)` for each retained dimension. | `0` to `1` for each dimension. | Indicates how much retained low-dimensional variance is represented by a given ENA dimension. Higher values mean that dimension captures more of the retained structure. |
| Mean Network Edge Weight | Arithmetic mean of each edge weight across all units in a group. | No fixed universal range; depends on normalization and data structure. | Describes the representative co-occurrence structure of a group. Larger positive edge weights indicate stronger co-occurrence relationships. |
| Subtracted Mean Network Edge Weight | `Group A mean edge weight - Group B mean edge weight` for each edge. | No fixed universal range; can be positive or negative. | Positive values indicate stronger connections for Group A; negative values indicate stronger connections for Group B. Larger absolute values indicate more salient differences. |
| Top Edge Differences | Rank-ordering of the largest positive and negative edge differences in the subtracted mean network. | No fixed universal range; depends on edge weights. | Identifies the most important edges driving group differences in the ENA comparison. |
| Axis Interpretation | Heuristic summary based on the node positions at the positive and negative poles of each dimension. | Not a numeric range in itself; derived from node coordinates. | Helps explain what each ENA dimension appears to distinguish. It should be used as interpretive support, not as a standalone statistical test. |

## Notes

In ENA, the most important distinction is between indicators that describe **projected point separation** and indicators that describe **network structure**. Point-based tests such as Welch t-test, Mann-Whitney *U*, and ANOVA evaluate whether groups differ in their positions in ENA space. Network-based indicators such as mean networks and subtracted networks explain which connections create that difference.

Chi-square should be interpreted separately from ENA-space statistics. It addresses whether the frequencies of codes differ between groups, whereas ENA tests address whether the **structure of co-occurrence relationships** differs between groups.

Goodness of fit should also be interpreted separately from group separation. A high goodness-of-fit value indicates that the visualization faithfully represents the underlying ENA model, not that the two groups are necessarily far apart.
